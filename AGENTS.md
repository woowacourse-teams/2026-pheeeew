# Project instruction

- Write Korean results reports so the reader can understand what was checked, what happened, and what remains without interpreting raw logs.
- Begin with the overall status and the most important outcomes. Organize details by scenario or question, using short bullets and a compact comparison table only when useful.
- Explain technical terms and past failures in plain language when they matter. Include conditions, sample size, and the meaning and limits of measurements; separate facts from interpretation and mark unknown or unmeasured items explicitly.
- Link to supporting evidence near each result, preserve original records, and avoid repeating raw data or presenting partial checks as completion.
- For issue comments, follow the user's instruction to prepare the content for review before posting and keep results to conditions, numbers, observations, and judgments.
- For measurement reports, start with a compact table whose rows are the user's metrics and whose columns compare the requested platforms or conditions. Show result values, sample sizes, or `미측정` directly in the cells; give only the minimum explanation needed to interpret them. Move detailed chronology and raw logs to linked evidence.
